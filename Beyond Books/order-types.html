Section 01

The 5 Order Types — Stripped Down

① Market Order
Execute now. At whatever price.

Fastest execution you can get. Zero price control. In options, slippage is real — you'll often get a worse fill than you expected because the spread is wide and you're competing against algos.

Fast No Price Control Slippage Risk
→ Use only when speed matters more than price. Exits in panic. Never for entries in options.
② Limit Order
Execute only at my price — or better.

Strong price control. No guaranteed execution. If price doesn't come to you, you don't trade. That's discipline, not a flaw.

Price Control Planned Entry Not Always Filled
→ Good for planned entries. Not for chasing a move that's already happened.
③ Stop Loss (SL vs SL-M)
Your exit when you're wrong.

SL (Limit-based): Trigger fires → limit order placed. Better price, but may not execute if price gaps through.
SL-M (Market-based): Trigger fires → market order executed. Guaranteed exit, will slip in fast moves.

SL → Price Control SL-M → Guaranteed Exit
→ In fast options markets, SL-M is usually more reliable. Don't get stuck holding because your SL didn't fill.
④ GTT — Good Till Triggered
A condition, not an order.

You're telling the system: "When price reaches X, place my order." GTT itself doesn't execute anything — it triggers an order. Critical distinction.

Single GTT → Used for entry at a level.
OCO GTT → Target + Stoploss together. When one fires, the other cancels.

Entry Tool OCO Exit Tool Does NOT Guarantee Fill
→ GTT guarantees order placement. Not execution. Don't confuse the two.
⑤ Bracket Order (BO)
Entry + SL + Target in one shot. In theory.

Looks clean on paper. But in fast markets it behaves unpredictably. Multiple triggers can fire, system risk increases. That's why most brokers restricted it.

Unpredictable in Volatility Most Brokers Restricted
→ Know it exists. Don't rely on it as a primary tool.

Section 03

7-Step Execution System

01

Pre-Market — Mark Your Levels

Define support, resistance, key zones before the bell rings. If levels aren't clear → No trade. Simple as that.

02

Define Your Trade Plan

Entry, SL, Target — written before market opens. Not improvised. Not adjusted mid-session based on hope.

03

Market Check at Open

Gap up/down? Volatility spiking? Levels respected? If the open is chaotic → avoid GTT entry. Let it settle.

04

Entry via GTT or Limit

Set trigger at 400, limit at 402–405. Avoid market orders in options unless it's an emergency exit.

05

⚡ Immediately After Fill — Place OCO

The moment your entry executes → OCO GTT goes on. Target 480. SL 320. Non-negotiable. Without this step, you are trading without protection.

06

Trade Management — Hands Off

Don't move SL randomly. Don't act on emotions. Let OCO do its job. Intervene only for news events, extreme volatility, or system issues.

07

Exit Handling

Target hits → profit booked. SL hits → loss controlled. Do not re-enter without a fresh setup. One trade, one clean cycle.
