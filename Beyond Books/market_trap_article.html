The Market Doesn't Move.
It Hunts.

Everything you think you know about support levels, breakouts, and "safe" entries is exactly what the market wants you to believe.

8-Chapter Guide  ·  Liquidity Traps  ·  Institutional Logic

By Sanjeev Singh  ·  Sanjeev Signals

Let's be honest. Most traders don't lose because they're dumb. They lose because they're playing a game they don't fully understand. They look at a chart, see a clean support level, and feel confident. That confidence? It's manufactured. The market built it on purpose.

Every "perfect" setup you've ever seen — that beautiful bounce off support, that FOMO-inducing breakout, that post-crash "buying opportunity" — was engineered to get you in at exactly the wrong moment. The market isn't chaotic. It's clinical. Its one job is to find your Stop Loss and trigger it.

This guide breaks down exactly how that happens — and more importantly, how you can stop being the liquidity and start trading it instead.

Chapter 01

Why the Market Needs You to Be Wrong

Here's something most retail traders never learn: the market physically cannot sustain a move if only one side is active. Think about it. If everyone is selling, there's no one left to buy from. The move stalls. It runs out of gas.

For a real, sustained move to happen — whether up or down — the market needs participants on both sides. Specifically, it needs to convince the wrong side to enter so their Stop Losses can fuel the next leg of the move.

A trap only works when the market successfully recruits the wrong side. The moment that side is fully committed — and proven wrong — their forced exits become the fuel for everything that happens next.

This is called the Dual Participation Principle, and it's the engine behind virtually every major market move. The professional trader isn't asking "where is price going?" They're asking "where has the market set up the most losing positions?" That's where price is going.

The Real Signal

When a chart looks "perfect" — when both a buyer and a seller could look at it and each feel 100% confident — that's not a gift. That's a red flag. The market is at its most dangerous when it's making the maximum number of participants feel secure.

Chapter 02

The Three Lures: How the Market Sets the Hook

The market uses three main tools to bait traders into bad positions. Once you see them, you can't unsee them.

Support and Resistance Levels

You buy at support because it "held before." The market built that level for exactly that reason — to cluster your Stop Loss just below it. Once enough buyers are in, those Stop Losses become sell orders that power the breakdown. The support level wasn't a floor. It was a trap door.

Round Numbers (The Psychological Magnets)

60,000. 59,000. 58,500. These numbers don't just matter to you — they matter to every retail trader in the world. The market knows this. It stages breakouts and breakdowns at these levels specifically to trigger FOMO entries at the exact point where a reversal is already planned.

Real World Example

The market breaks 60,000 with a massive green candle. FOMO buyers pile in. But here's the thing — by the time that breakout happened, every seller's Stop Loss above that level had already been cleared. There was no fuel left to push higher. A breakout that's already consumed its fuel is a reversal signal, not a continuation signal.

Closing Prices

Traders treat the previous day's close like it's sacred — fair value, the ultimate pivot. The market uses this. It hovers around closing prices to gauge volume and trap traders who are still reacting to yesterday's news. It's the connective tissue between two days of action, and it's one of the most reliable places to find a setup.

Pro Tip

These traps are exponentially more powerful when they line up across indices. If Bank Nifty is testing 59,000 while Sensex is hitting resistance near 79,000, you're not looking at one trap — you're looking at a coordinated ambush. Both sides, across the entire market, are being baited simultaneously.

Chapter 03

War, Bad News, and the Market's Cruel Paradox

Here's where things get genuinely counterintuitive. You'd assume that when terrible news breaks — geopolitical conflict, war, economic shock — the market would just fall. Straight down. Makes sense, right?

Wrong. And understanding why is one of the most important things you can learn as a trader.

In a negative news environment, retail buyers are terrified. They won't just walk in and buy a falling market. Which means if the market drops vertically, there's nobody on the other side — no buyers to provide the liquidity that big institutional players need to exit or position themselves properly.

So what does the market do? It creates support. Artificially. On purpose.

In a news-driven market, the appearance of "support" isn't a sign of strength. It's a recruitment tool designed to bring back the buyers that fear chased away — so their Stop Losses can be used as fuel for the next leg down.

The market builds a base. Maybe it holds a round number. Maybe it stages a brief rally. Traders who think "the worst is over" step back in. They feel comfortable. They set their stops. And then the floor disappears.

What This Means for Today

Every time you see "the market bouncing despite bad news," ask yourself: is this genuine strength, or is this the market recruiting buyers before the next move lower? In a heavy news cycle, the bounce is almost always a trap. The professionals know the public needs to see stability before they'll enter — and they build exactly that, on purpose, before they pull it away.

Chapter 04

Gaps Change Everything: The New Chart Protocol

When the market opens with a massive gap — up or down — it's like a circuit breaker on your entire chart. Yesterday's levels? Largely irrelevant. The traders who were positioned based on yesterday's structure are already either in deep profit or deep pain. Their Stop Losses have either been hit or are so far away they're not active liquidity anymore.

The "old chart" is dead. You need to follow the new one.

What to Do After a Big Gap

  1. Give it 15–30 minutes. Don't trade the open. The first few candles are chaos — profit-booking, panic, repositioning. Wait for the structure to emerge.
  2. Anchor to round numbers. When old structure is gone, the market gravitates toward major psychological levels (60,000, 59,000, etc.). These become your new reference points.
  3. Compare your indices. If one index is testing its previous close while another is still far away, that divergence matters. It often precedes a reversal.
The Recovery Candle Trap

After a big gap down, you'll almost always see a large green candle. Don't chase it. That candle is a weapon designed to scare away direct sellers and lure in "recovery" buyers — who then get trapped when the market eventually breaks back below the key level. The green candle creates a false sense of safety right before the floor drops.

Chapter 05

Connecting the Dots: How Yesterday Sets Up Today

Professional traders don't look at today's chart in isolation. Every session is a continuation of the previous one — the trapped positions, the carried-over fears, the psychological anchors from yesterday's close are all still alive. They're the raw material the market uses to manufacture today's move.

Think of it this way: when you carry a position overnight, you're carrying emotion with it. The market knows where those emotions are. It knows where you'll panic, where you'll cover, where you'll finally give up. And it moves toward those points systematically.

Two Classic Setups to Know

The Support Trap (Bearish Setup): In a bad news environment, the market can't just drop vertically — nobody will buy into a freefall. So it builds a visible support level, usually near a round number. Value-seekers step in. Their stops cluster below. Then the floor gives way and those stops become the fuel for the real move.

The Resistance Trap (Bullish Setup): After heavy selling, nobody wants to buy. So the market takes resistance from a round number or the previous close — inviting sellers who think the downtrend is continuing. When it breaks above that resistance, it isn't just "going up." It's being propelled by the forced covering of those trapped sellers.

The Golden Rule

Market sustainability depends entirely on two-sided participation. If only one side is active, the move will fail. Before you enter any trade, ask yourself: where's the other side? If you can't identify where the opposing participants are being invited in — there's no trade.

Chapter 06

The Retail Fallacies That Keep Costing You Money

There are a few mental traps that retail traders fall into over and over again. The market doesn't just know about these — it exploits them.

"It's fallen too much — it has to bounce."

This is probably the most expensive sentence in retail trading. The market can always go further. When you assume a bottom based on how far something has fallen, you're providing the exact buy-side liquidity that institutions need to exit their short positions at the best price. You're not catching a knife. You're handing it to someone else and asking them to put it down gently.

"I missed the move — I'll wait for the bounce and enter."

When a market falls directly without you, the natural instinct is to wait for a retracement and enter on the pullback. The market uses this. Those small retracements — those "safe entry" moments — are often liquidity-harvesting operations designed to trap late arrivals right before the next leg continues.

"The news is terrible, so the market must go down."

This feels logical. It isn't. As we covered in Chapter 3, the market often needs to show strength in bad news environments just to recruit enough buyers to fuel the next move lower. Terrible news and a falling market aren't as correlated as they feel. The market moves on liquidity, not headlines.

The Bottom Line

Institutional logic is frequently the inverse of what retail traders consider "common sense." If your trade thesis sounds like something a beginner would feel safe doing — buying obvious support, shorting obvious resistance, entering on a clean breakout — you might want to think twice before hitting confirm.

Chapter 07

Fridays, Holidays, and the Weekend Trap

Market dynamics shift in a specific, predictable way on Friday afternoons and before major holidays. Retail traders don't want to hold heavy positions over a two-day weekend — too much can happen. Too many gaps can open. So they start reducing.

This creates a liquidity vacuum. And institutional players love a vacuum.

With retail volume reduced, big players can move the market with far less resistance. They can hit stops, push through levels, and reposition — all with precision — because the usual retail "friction" simply isn't there. This is why you often see outsized, seemingly random moves late on Friday afternoons. It's not random. It's professional traders operating in a market that's temporarily cleared of the usual noise.

Remember This

If a major move happens in the last hour of a Friday session — especially into a long weekend — be very skeptical of it as a directional signal. It's often a liquidation event, not a trend change. The big players are cleaning up retail positions before they go home for the weekend.

Chapter 08

Before Every Trade: Your Trap-Detection Checklist

A professional trader doesn't try to catch every move in a session. That's an ego trap, not a trading strategy. The goal is to identify one high-conviction setup where the trap is visible and the reaction is tradeable. Before you enter anything, run through this:

  • Dual Participation Check — Are both buyers and sellers being invited to this level? If only one side is engaged, a trap can't form — and the move won't sustain.
  • The Magnet Check — Is there a round number or closing price nearby? Is the market stalling there deliberately to collect more orders before moving?
  • Bias Check — Am I entering because "it's moved too much"? That's not analysis. The market can always go further in either direction.
  • Lure Identification — In a bad news environment, is the market building a "support" base just to make me comfortable buying? Is this strength real or manufactured?
  • Gap Logic — Am I trading based on today's fresh price action, or am I anchored to levels from yesterday that no longer matter?
  • Friday Factor — Am I aware that weekend risk changes how the market behaves? Late Friday moves deserve extra skepticism.
  • Exit Clarity — Do I have a specific logical target where the trade is done? Once you've caught the reaction from a trap, your work is finished. Get out.
The Professional Mandate

Only commit capital after the trap has been fully set. Patience isn't passive — it's the active work of waiting for the market to finish luring the crowd. High-probability trades don't exist in isolation. They exist precisely at the moment of retail liquidation. Your job is to align your execution with the harvest, not the invitation.

Are You the Trapper or the Trapped?

This entire guide comes down to one shift in perspective: stop looking at price and start looking at people. The market moves because a group of participants is being forced to liquidate a losing position. Find those people, understand their pain, and you'll know where price is going before it gets there.

Charts aren't patterns. They're psychology made visible. Every "support level" is a promise the market made to someone it's about to break. Every "clean breakout" is bait for the most impulsive traders in the room. Every rally in a falling market is a last chance to create the buyers that will fund the next leg down.

Once you see the market this way — as a machine designed to manufacture and extract liquidity — you stop being the liquidity. And that changes everything.

"Patience beats noise. Every single time."
