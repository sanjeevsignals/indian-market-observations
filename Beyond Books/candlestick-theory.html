12 Core Principles · Order Flow Framework

Sanjeev Singh

01
Foundation
Core Principle: Candles Reflect Order Flow

A candle is not a pattern. It is the visible footprint of aggression + absorption + rejection.

Core Q
Every candle answers one question: Who controlled the close?

Every price action analysis must follow a structured framework:

Level
→
Reaction
→
Close
→
Follow-through
02
Structure
Candle Anatomy Model
Strong Bull
Large Body
Strong Bear
Large Body
Doji
Indecision
Supply Wick
Rejection
Demand Wick
Rejection
Body
  • Large body = Aggressive participation
  • Small body = Indecision / equilibrium
Wick
  • Upper wick = Supply presence
  • Lower wick = Demand presence
  • Long wick = Liquidity sweep or rejection
Wick without level = noise
Wick at key level = information
03
Control
Close Dominance Rule
↑ Bullish Close
  • Close near high
  • Bullish strength confirmed
  • Buyers controlled session
↓ Bearish Close
  • Close near low
  • Bearish strength confirmed
  • Sellers controlled session
Mid-close = Lack of commitment. Avoid directional bias.

The close defines session control. The next candle confirms continuation or failure.

04
Context
Location Over Pattern Rule

Same candle carries different meaning depending on location:

At Support
  • Bullish engulfing = potential reversal
  • High probability setup
At Resistance
  • Bullish engulfing = possible bull trap
  • Low probability setup
Rule
Structure > Pattern. Always.
05
Bias Shift
Break of Structure Confirmation

A single candle does not shift bias. Bias shifts only when all three conditions are met:

  1. Structure breaks
  2. Close sustains beyond level
  3. Pullback respects new structure
Without all three conditions → No directional commitment.
06
Traps
Rejection & Liquidity Theory

Long wick beyond a key level often signals:

What Happened
  • Stop hunt completed
  • Breakout traders trapped
  • Inventory transferred
Entry Logic
  • Wait for reclaim of level
  • Enter on pullback
  • Invalidate beyond wick extreme
07
Patterns
Engulfing Candle Logic
Bullish Engulfing
  • Absorption of prior supply
  • Must break minor structure
Bearish Engulfing
  • Absorption of prior demand
  • Must break minor structure
If no structure break → It's just volatility. Do not trade it.
08
Volatility
Compression → Expansion Model

Inside bars and small candles represent a phase of energy accumulation:

Compression Phase
  • Volatility contraction
  • Energy buildup
  • Inside bars / doji clusters
Expansion Phase
  • Follows compression
  • Trade breakout only
  • Must align with HTF bias
Compression
→
Energy Buildup
→
Expansion
→
HTF Aligned
09
Filter
Momentum vs Exhaustion Filter
Momentum Signs
  • Consecutive strong bodies
  • Minimal opposing wicks
  • Strong closes
Exhaustion Signs
  • Large candle after extended move
  • Increasing upper/lower wicks
  • Slowing follow-through
Late entry after expansion = poor reward positioning.
10
Alignment
Multi-Timeframe Alignment
HTF
Higher Timeframe — Bias Control
MTF
Mid Timeframe — Structure Confirmation
LTF
Lower Timeframe — Entry Precision
If LTF signal contradicts HTF structure → Avoid the trade entirely.
Hierarchy: HTF Bias → MTF Structure → LTF Trigger
11
Protection
Risk & Invalidation Framework

Every entry must define three non-negotiable elements:

  1. Logical stop — placed beyond structure or wick
  2. Minimum 1:2 Risk-to-Reward ratio
  3. Position size based on invalidation distance
Rule
No clear invalidation = No trade. Period.
12
Execution
Decision Checklist (Pre-Entry)

Before executing any trade, ask all six questions:

  • 1
    Where is price relative to the key level?
  • 2
    Who controlled the close?
  • 3
    Has structure shifted?
  • 4
    Is liquidity taken?
  • 5
    Is the HTF aligned?
  • 6
    Is the Risk-to-Reward acceptable?
Final
If any answer is unclear — Stand aside. Patience is a position.
