What Is CPR — And Why Is It a Leading Indicator?

Most indicators follow price. CPR anticipates it.

CPR (Central Pivot Range) uses yesterday's session data to construct today's key levels before the market opens. You have your map before the first candle prints — that structural advantage is what separates it from every reactive indicator on your chart.

While moving averages and oscillators respond to price that has already moved, CPR delivers pre-calculated zones of support, resistance, and indecision. That forward-looking nature is precisely what classifies it as a leading indicator.

02

Calculation

Three numbers from the prior session. That is the entire input.

Pivot — P
( H + L + C ) ÷ 3
BC — Bottom Central
( H + L ) ÷ 2
TC — Top Central
( P − BC ) + P

Input: Prev Day High · Low · Close  →  Output: TC · Pivot · BC — three levels available before market open.

These three levels form the CPR band. Not merely a set of lines — a representation of the market's fair value zone from the prior session.

04

Opening Bias — Reading the Day's Context

The single most important observation: where does price open relative to the CPR band? That one fact defines your entire directional stance for the session before a single trade is placed.

Bullish
Open above TC
Buyers are in control from the open. Look for BUY setups exclusively. Fading strength here carries low probability without exceptional confirmation.
Bearish
Open below BC
Sellers are in control from the open. Look for SELL setups exclusively. Attempting longs against this context requires extraordinary evidence.
Neutral
Open inside CPR
No directional edge. The market hasn't declared intent. Avoid trading — wait for price to exit the band and confirm a side.
05

CPR Width — Reading the Market's Mood

The distance between TC and BC tells you what kind of day to expect before it begins. This is one of CPR's most underused signals.

Narrow CPR
Trending Day

TC and BC are very close. Low prior-session volatility compresses the band. The market is coiling — a large directional move is expected. This is your highest-probability trading day.

Wide CPR
Range-Bound Day

TC and BC are far apart. High prior-session volatility expands the band. Choppy, contained movement is the likely outcome. Scalp lightly or sit out entirely.

Pine Script — PRO+ Strategy Code

A complete TradingView strategy implementation. Plots TC, Pivot, and BC; assigns directional bias from opening price; calculates ATR-based stops and targets; generates entry signals and configurable alerts.

Pine Script v5 · CPR by Sanjeev Signals PRO+ 
//@version=5
strategy("CPR by Sanjeev Signals PRO+", overlay=true,
  default_qty_type=strategy.percent_of_equity, default_qty_value=10)

// === Previous Day Data ===
prevHigh  = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", high[1])
prevLow   = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", low[1])
prevClose = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", close[1])
todayOpen = request.security(syminfo.tickerid, "D", open)

// === CPR Calculation ===
pivot = (prevHigh + prevLow + prevClose) / 3
bc    = (prevHigh + prevLow) / 2
tc    = (pivot - bc) + pivot

// === Plot CPR Lines ===
plot(tc,    color=color.red,    linewidth=2, title="TC")
plot(pivot, color=color.yellow, linewidth=2, title="Pivot")
plot(bc,    color=color.green,  linewidth=2, title="BC")

// === Bias ===
bullish  = todayOpen > tc
bearish  = todayOpen < bc

// === Background Color ===
bgcolor(bullish ? color.new(color.green, 90) :
        bearish ? color.new(color.red,   90) : na)

// === Narrow CPR Detection ===
cprWidth  = tc - bc
avgWidth  = ta.sma(cprWidth, 10)
narrowCPR = cprWidth < avgWidth

// === ATR-based SL & Target ===
atr         = ta.atr(14)
longSL      = close - atr
longTarget  = close + (2 * atr)
shortSL     = close + atr
shortTarget = close - (2 * atr)

// === Entry Conditions ===
longEntry  = bullish and close > high[1]
shortEntry = bearish and close < low[1]

// === Execute Strategy ===
if (longEntry)
    strategy.entry("BUY", strategy.long)
    strategy.exit("TP/SL BUY", from_entry="BUY", stop=longSL, limit=longTarget)

if (shortEntry)
    strategy.entry("SELL", strategy.short)
    strategy.exit("TP/SL SELL", from_entry="SELL", stop=shortSL, limit=shortTarget)

// === Visual Signals ===
plotshape(longEntry,  location=location.belowbar, color=color.green,
  style=shape.labelup,   text="BUY")
plotshape(shortEntry, location=location.abovebar, color=color.red,
  style=shape.labeldown, text="SELL")

// === Alerts ===
alertcondition(longEntry,  title="Buy Alert",  message="CPR BUY — Sanjeev Signals")
alertcondition(shortEntry, title="Sell Alert", message="CPR SELL — Sanjeev Signals")

Market Psychology Embedded in CPR

CPR is not purely mathematical. The three inputs — prior day High, Low, and Close — carry behavioral information about every participant who traded that session.

Previous High & Low

The outer limits of buyer and seller aggression — where conviction ran out and participants reversed or stepped away.

Previous Close

The final consensus. Where the entire market agreed to end the session — the last statement before tomorrow's open.

What CPR Captures

The fair value zone. Between BC and TC is where price was broadly accepted and traded, not merely reached.

What It Implies

Where imbalance may resurface. CPR bridges past behavior and future expectation — that's where the structural edge originates.

10

CPR Is a Framework — Not a Signal

Price above CPR does not mean buy. Price below CPR does not mean sell. That approach bleeds capital consistently.

The most persistent mistake among developing traders is mechanizing CPR into a signal generator. The levels don't fire entries. They provide structure, context, and areas of interest. The trade itself — the entry — must be earned through price action, volume, and market structure.

  • Context — defines whether you're operating in a bullish, bearish, or neutral environment before any other analysis
  • Structure — creates clear reference lines that price will either respect decisively or break through with force
  • Areas of Interest — TC, Pivot, and BC are zones to monitor, not automatic trade triggers
11

Integrating CPR with Price Action

The framework's real power surfaces when combined with how price actually behaves around those levels. The levels are inert without behavioral context — the behavior is where the trade lives.

  • Breakout above CPR with strong volume — genuine strength; institutional participation is likely behind the move
  • Rejection from CPR — resistance is holding; a reversal or fade opportunity begins to build here
  • Consolidation within CPR — market indecision; no directional edge; step aside and wait for resolution
  • Price accepting and closing above TC — sustained closes, not just spikes; confirms bullish institutional intent
  • Price failing to reclaim BC from below — confirms bearish control; breakdowns and fades become higher probability
12

Risk Management — Structurally Defined Stops

One underappreciated property of this framework: CPR naturally provides structurally logical stop-loss placement. Not arbitrary ATR multiples — levels the market itself has defined as meaningful.

Bullish Trade — Stop Placement

Stop goes below BC. If price cannot hold the lower boundary of the prior session's fair value zone, the bullish thesis is structurally invalidated.

Bearish Trade — Stop Placement

Stop goes above TC. If price reclaims the upper boundary of CPR, the bearish case no longer has structural support.

Invalidation, not guesswork. CPR defines clear structural levels where the trade thesis fails. Knowing exactly when you're wrong — before you enter — is the foundation of every sound risk model.

13

Limitations

No framework survives contact with the market without an honest accounting of what it cannot do. Applying CPR with blind confidence is as dangerous as ignoring it entirely.

  • —CPR cannot predict news-driven moves — macro events, earnings releases, and policy decisions override every technical level without warning
  • —It does not guarantee direction — opening above TC still requires price action confirmation before any trade is justified
  • —It requires behavioral context — CPR levels without price action reading are just lines; they carry no predictive value in isolation
  • —Blind reliance without context generates false setups — especially during gap opens, high-volatility sessions, and news-driven days
14

Advanced Edge — What Most Traders Miss

Virgin CPR

A prior session's CPR that price never touched. It functions as a strong magnetic level in subsequent sessions — price has a demonstrated tendency to revisit it.

CPR Shift

Today's CPR is positioned higher than yesterday's — a trend continuation signal. The market is printing higher value zones. Trade with that momentum, not against it.

Price Acceptance

Sustained trading and closing above TC signals institutional accumulation. Not a spike — acceptance. This is the framework's strongest and most reliable setup.
